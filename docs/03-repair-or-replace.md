# Repair-or-Replace Demands

## What it is

When a product fails and a repair doesn't fix it, you can demand that the company repair it properly, replace it, or refund your money. Some states have "lemon laws" for cars, and the federal Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act covers written warranties on consumer products.

## Before you start

Gather:

- Repair invoices and work orders
- Dates of each repair attempt
- Photos showing the problem still exists
- The warranty or service contract

## Step-by-step

1. **Count repair attempts.** Many states consider a product a "lemon" after a set number of failed repairs (often 3–4) or days out of service.
2. **Put the demand in writing.** A verbal request is easy to ignore; a letter creates a paper trail.
3. **Send by a trackable method.** Certified mail or email with a read receipt gives you proof of delivery.
4. **Set a deadline.** Give the company a reasonable time to respond (often 14–30 days).
5. **Keep copies.** Save everything you send and receive.

## Checklist

- [ ] Repair dates and invoices collected
- [ ] Number of failed repair attempts counted
- [ ] Written demand letter sent with a deadline
- [ ] Proof of delivery saved
- [ ] Calendar reminder set for the response deadline

## When to get help

If the product is a vehicle, or the amount is large, talk to a consumer attorney. Many offer free consultations, and some cases qualify for fee-shifting (the company pays your lawyer if you win).
