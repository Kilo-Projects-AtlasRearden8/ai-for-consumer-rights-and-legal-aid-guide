# Small-Claims Court

## What it is

Small-claims court lets you sue for a limited amount of money without a lawyer. Limits vary by state (often $2,500–$10,000). You present your case to a judge, and the other side presents theirs.

## Before you start

- Confirm your claim is within your state's small-claims limit
- Check the filing deadline (statute of limitations) — it varies by state and claim type
- Know the correct court and filing fee (some courts waive fees if you can't afford them)
- Gather evidence: receipts, contracts, photos, letters, messages

## Step-by-step

1. **Send a demand letter first.** Some courts require it; it's smart even when not required.
2. **File the complaint.** Fill out the court's small-claims form and pay the fee (or ask for a fee waiver).
3. **Serve the defendant.** The court will tell you how to deliver papers to the other side.
4. **Prepare for the hearing.** Organize evidence, write out what you'll say, practice.
5. **Attend the hearing.** Bring copies of everything for the judge and the other side.
6. **Collect if you win.** Winning the judgment doesn't always mean easy collection.

## Checklist

- [ ] Claim amount within state small-claims limit
- [ ] Filing deadline checked
- [ ] Demand letter sent
- [ ] Court forms completed
- [ ] Filing fee paid or waiver requested
- [ ] Defendant served
- [ ] Evidence organized for hearing

## When to get help

Small-claims is designed for people without lawyers, but court clerks cannot give legal advice. For help, contact legal aid, a law school clinic, or a consumer attorney. Court self-help centers may offer free assistance.
