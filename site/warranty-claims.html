Warranty Claims

A warranty is a promise from a manufacturer or seller that a product will work for a certain time. If it breaks during that time, you can ask for a repair, replacement, or refund.

Two main types

  • Express warranty: written promises in paperwork, ads, or on the box
  • Implied warranty: the basic idea that a product should work as expected (rules vary by state)

Before you start

  • The receipt or proof of purchase
  • The warranty document (paper or online)
  • Photos of the defect
  • The product model and serial number
  • Dates of purchase and when the problem started

Step-by-step

  1. Check the warranty period. Is the product still covered?
  2. Read what's covered. Some warranties exclude accidental damage or normal wear and tear.
  3. Find the claims process. Many warranties require you to register or call a specific number first.
  4. Contact the seller or manufacturer. Ask for repair, replacement, or refund.
  5. Keep records. Save emails, chat transcripts, and notes of phone calls (date, time, who you spoke with).
  6. Escalate if ignored. Send a written demand letter (see the templates page).

AI prompts to try

  • "Help me write a polite warranty claim letter to [MANUFACTURER] for a [PRODUCT] bought on [DATE] that stopped working on [DATE]. Include the model number [MODEL] and request repair or replacement under the warranty."
  • "What should I ask customer service before I send a warranty claim?"

Checklist

  • Receipt located
  • Warranty terms read
  • Photos taken of the defect
  • First contact made (date recorded)
  • Written follow-up sent if no resolution
  • Copies of all correspondence saved

When to get help

If the claim involves a major appliance or vehicle, a large amount of money, or the company denies a clearly covered claim, contact your state consumer-protection office or legal aid.
